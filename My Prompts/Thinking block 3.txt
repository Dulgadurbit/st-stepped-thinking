First Instruction: Calibrated Reactions
People are self-interested, but they are also socially calibrated. They pursue their wants in ways that protect their relationships, match the tone of the room, and maintain their dignity. They do not act on raw, unfiltered impulse unless in extreme distress.

Given their Physical body, Contextual Interpretation, Active needs, and Social Filter:

1.1 What does the character want to achieve in this immediate moment that aligns with the ACTIVE (not dormant) needs? (e.g., "She wants to playfully demand his attention and participate in the teasing game.")
1.2 How does the character modulate this want to match the subtext of the scene? If the scene is playful, how do they play along? If the scene is serious, how do they act accordingly? 
1.3 What do they believe they can realistically get right now without breaking the social contract or making things weird? 
1.4 Do they need to say something, or can it be clearly implied by actions?
1.5 What is the proportional physical and emotional intensity for this reaction? (Scale of 1-10, where 1 is a subtle glance and 10 is a life-or-death struggle. Justify the number. A playful tease should rarely exceed a 3 or 4).

Third Instruction: Dialogue Check
If any planned action involves speech:

3.1 What is the SURFACE CONTENT of what they say? (The literal topic, story, question, or remark.)
3.2 What is the SOCIAL FUNCTION of saying it? (To fill silence? To redirect? To test a reaction? To show off? To bond? To provoke? To deflect?)

These two must NOT be the same thing. If the surface content and the social function are identical, the dialogue is on-the-nose. Rewrite until they are different layers.
(e.g., Surface: "So how was the trip?" / Function: Testing whether he'll bring up the argument they had before he left.)

If no planned action involves speech, write "No dialogue planned" and move on.
